# Video auto-sync verification

The prototype displays candidates only. Automatic application remains an unmet requirement: independent precision anchors, held-out positives and negatives, and evidence of continuity across edits are insufficient to establish a safe threshold. A candidate's rank, correlation, ambiguity flag and peak width do not establish correctness; peak width is a heuristic rather than a confidence interval. Manual alignment remains available.

## Real recording investigation

On 2026-10-30 an Apple M3 MacBook Air (16 GB, macOS 27.0.1) was used with the existing cached 2636.6-second video and the two Purdue GP XRKs. The footage is edited at pit stops. No new download or algorithm tuning was used. A 1/10 fps contact sheet shows pit-lane imagery around 1205–1215 s and track driving around 1225 s, but does not establish a cut boundary or continuity.

Independent of the matcher, eleven checkerboard-stripe approaches were visually sampled at 2 fps between approximately 1252.75 and 1525.75 s. Their intervals closely follow the 0362 XRK's complete lap boundary sequence, including a longer fourth interval. Pairing the sequences gives a mean telemetry-minus-video offset of **-1211.410 s**, with residuals from -0.210 to +0.275 s. The corresponding 0361 sequence gives -1199.405 s and wider residuals (-0.931 to +0.858 s), making it a weaker-matching control; both recordings occur somewhere in the video, so 0361 is not a proven unrelated negative.

The samples show the stripe near the foreground, not exact wheel crossings. Visual timing has at least ±0.5 s uncertainty, and the systematic difference between the stripe, the XRK lap trigger and camera/vehicle positions is unbounded. The sequence is an approximate identification, not calibrated ground truth. It has not been split into independent held-out anchors, and no independent 0361 offset, unrelated negative or verified cut continuity was established. The user also identifies the fastest 26.5 s lap start at video 23:37 ±1 s. If this is the 0362 lap beginning at telemetry 206.530 s (duration 26.585 s), the nominal telemetry-minus-video offset is -1210.470 s. The updated matcher estimate of -1211.80 s differs by -1.33 s from that nominal offset; the absolute difference is approximately 0.33–2.33 s under the stated ±1 s assumption. This comparison is conditional on lap identity and assumes the user’s time refers to the telemetry lap start; it is not a general accuracy bound. The 0361 fastest lap is 26.676 s, so the rounded duration alone does not establish identity.

## Current matcher and decoder results

The matcher now refines qualifying coarse peaks before ranking them using correlation, overlap and support from separated telemetry-yaw episodes, with a penalty for contradictory windows. It retains up to 256 distinct peaks before returning eight; exceeding that capacity reports an incomplete competitor comparison. The 0.3 coarse-correlation gate and ranking thresholds are heuristics. Every nonempty result remains unresolved and automatic application is disabled. Temporal episodes do not prove independent physical corners: the matcher lacks validated corner regions and per-fix accuracy and lap provenance. GPS uncertainty can split or merge spatial regions, and a long turn can still contain multiple noise bursts.

Using the saved 1250–1528 s motion signature and current telemetry signatures, without another decode, the updated 0362 top candidate is **-1211.80 s**, correlation 0.3353, overlap 244.6 s, sign +1. Its turning windows are inconsistent, the result is ambiguous and inapplicable, and the previous 11.2 s edge at -949.00 s is now eighth. The 0361 top candidate is -1391.40 s, correlation 0.3578, overlap 73.6 s, sign +1, also inconsistent, ambiguous and inapplicable. 0361 is not an unrelated negative. These are developmental comparisons on the same data, not held-out validation.

On macOS, the decoder now starts AVFoundation reading one second before the requested first target (rounded down to 1/600 s), decodes forward, and preserves sample presentation timestamps. For the same 0362 source and interval, 1,390 frames took 34.249 s total, with sampling completed at 34.002 s; the earlier sequential-from-start run took 105.354 s total, with sampling completed at 105.114 s. These are single uncontrolled measurements, not a benchmark distribution. A nonzero AVFoundation track start, unusual codec/GOP behavior, Windows native execution, and hard cancellation latency during native initialization or decoding remain unverified.

Synthetic checks do not establish a real-world false-positive rate. Existing tests reject stationary/zero-quality samples and preserve repeated-lap alternatives as ambiguous, including across chunks. A simulated cut is a gap in yaw samples; its test only asserts that the result is inapplicable, and does not prove detection of a real edit. Three deterministic synthetic independent white-noise telemetry sequences against the saved real motion signature yielded zero, zero and two candidates respectively; the third produced a short 10.6 s candidate with correlation 0.464, but all three were inapplicable. This small sample does not characterize unrelated footage.

## Lap-sequence redesign (2026-10-30)

The workspace now uses continuous-clip lap sequences as its primary evidence. A 320×180 grayscale pass at 0.4 s cadence retains normalized OpenCV scene descriptors, checker-pattern salience, road-region tracking support and media presentation timestamps. Abrupt coarse changes are proposals only: 0.05 s neighborhoods must show failed road continuity, changed appearance and a stable following frame before splitting a clip. Continuity support excludes the lower cockpit and central helmet regions. Repeated views and XRK-conditioned checker-peak chains produce separate hypotheses. Only promising neighborhoods are revisited at 0.1 s; features are shared across imported recordings. Searches and candidate lists are bounded, with incomplete comparisons exposed. Existing 599 s analysis bounds remain.

Sequence matching requires at least three intervals, aggregates adjacent complete laps for missing visual crossings, breaks at incomplete/disconnected telemetry laps, and estimates separate offsets per clip. Ranking retains competing recordings/phases and representation across clips. Checker-supported candidates receive bounded local GPS/motion refinement; raw sequence mappings and proposed motion displacements remain separate. Generic landmarks have unknown phase and do not receive misleading local trigger refinement. If no sequence exists, the selected recording retains motion-only fallback candidates using the coarse samples. Manual segments are never modified and automatic application remains disabled.

The first coarse cut implementation falsely split ordinary turns; this was corrected before integration. Generic repeated scenes also ranked a corner at −1217.317 s ahead of the stripe. A short diagnostic established temporal aliasing: checker salience peaked at 0.188 near 1252.65 s, while surrounding 0.4 s samples scored 0.076–0.109. Soft visual peaks now propose bounded finer neighborhoods. XRK intervals help select marker hypotheses, so their residuals are conditional fit, **not independent identification confidence or absolute timing error**. The leading marker confidence is always explicitly “XRK-conditioned; unvalidated and ambiguous.”
